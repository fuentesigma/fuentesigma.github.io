A Stokes law for connections that permute

2025 – 2026

Attach to each vertex of a 2-cell complex an unordered set of N states, couple neighbouring sets by an entropic optimal transport problem, and read the holonomy of the discrete connection that results. The holonomy of a boundary is a tree-ordered product of the holonomies of the faces it encloses. Its abelianisation gives a sum rule on every closed oriented surface, and on the torus a gauge-invariant signature in the first \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-cohomology.

Fibres without labels

Let \(\SN\) be the symmetric group on \(N\) elements. A vector bundle attaches a vector space to each point of a base and takes its structure group inside the invertible matrices. The bundle considered here attaches instead a finite set of \(N\) states carrying no order among them, and the structure group is \(\SN\). A labelling of the states is a choice made at one point, and whether that choice propagates consistently over the base is the question the construction answers.

The base is a connected neighbourhood graph \(G=(V,E)\) carrying a 2-cell structure. Over each vertex sits a multiset of \(N\) jets, each jet a value together with a first derivative, and the associated empirical measure on the jet space has uniform weights. Two jets attached to different vertices are compared by a metric weighing values and derivatives separately, with the derivative carried between tangent spaces by parallel transport before the comparison is made.

eq. 1 \[ d_{\mathcal Z}^2\big((x,z),(x',z')\big) = \lambda_{\mathrm{val}}\,\|y-y'\|^2 + \lambda_{\mathrm{jac}}\,\big\|J - J'\circ \mathrm{PT}_{x\to x'}\big\|_{\HS}^2 , \qquad z=(y,J). \]

The weights \(\lambda_{\mathrm{val}}\) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{jac}}\) are non-negative and not both zero. Carrying the derivative rather than the value alone is what makes the comparison rigid, since states agreeing in value across a region are still separated by the behaviour of their derivatives there.

On each oriented edge \(x_\alpha\to x_\beta\) the entropic optimal transport problem between the two empirical fibres, with eq. 1 as ground cost and uniform marginals, has a unique minimiser \(\Pi^{(\varepsilon)}_{\alpha\beta}\) at regularisation \(\varepsilon > 0\) [1, 2]. The edge transport is its normalisation.

eq. 2 \[ T_{\alpha\beta} := N\,\Pi^{(\varepsilon)}_{\alpha\beta}, \qquad T_{\alpha\beta}\ \text{doubly stochastic}. \]

The family \(\{T_{\alpha\beta}\}\) is a discrete connection, defined up to relabelling of the states at each vertex. A relabelling acts by \(T_{\alpha\beta}\mapsto P_\beta\,T_{\alpha\beta}\,P_\alpha^{\top}\) with \(P_\alpha\) and \(P_\beta\) permutation matrices, and every quantity below is required to be invariant under that action.

Three regimes are distinguished by the treatment of \(\varepsilon\), and they differ in the algebraic structure their edge transports carry. Regime (A) holds \(\varepsilon > 0\) fixed, where each \(T_{\alpha\beta}\) is doubly stochastic and need not be invertible, and loop products are linear maps on probability vectors without a group structure. Regime (B) sends \(\varepsilon\downarrow 0\). Regime (C) bypasses the regularisation and solves the linear assignment problem exactly on each edge, giving \(T_{\alpha\beta}\in\SN\) and loop products in \(\SN\). Group language is reserved for regime (C).

Holonomy and its invariants

A based loop \(\gamma=(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_L=\alpha_0)\) carries the ordered product of its edge transports.

eq. 3 \[ \Hol_\gamma := T_{\alpha_{L-1}\alpha_L}\cdots T_{\alpha_1\alpha_2}\,T_{\alpha_0\alpha_1}. \]

For a connected graph the fundamental group \(\pi_1(G,x_0)\) is free on \(r=|E|-|V|+1\) generators, one for each edge outside a chosen spanning tree. The assignment \([\gamma]\mapsto\Hol_\gamma\) is a homomorphism out of the opposite group, valued in \(\SN\) in regime (C) and in the endomorphisms of \(\mathbb R^N\) in regime (A). A relabelling at the basepoint conjugates it, and the representation is therefore well defined up to conjugation.

Two functions extract the conjugation-invariant content. The character spectrum records the cycle type in full, and the abelianisation records its parity alone.

eq. 4 \[ p_k(\gamma) := \frac{1}{N}\tr\!\left(\Hol_\gamma^{\,k}\right), \quad k=1,\dots,N-1, \qquad \eta(\gamma) := \operatorname{sign}\det \Hol_\gamma \in \{\pm 1\}. \]

In regime (C) the quantity \(p_k(\gamma)\) equals the fraction of fixed points of \(\Hol_\gamma^{\,k}\) and lies in \([-1,1]\). The tuple \((p_1,\dots,p_{N-1})\) is a class function on \(\SN\), and by Newton's identities it determines the cycle type and therefore the conjugacy class of \(\Hol_\gamma\). Since \(\pi_1(G,x_0)\) is free on \(r\) generators, evaluating the tuple on those generators is a finite test rather than a statement quantified over all loops. In regime (A) the \(p_k(\gamma)\) are normalised power sums of the spectrum of the Markov operator \(\Hol_\gamma\), complex in general and bounded by one in modulus. The map \(\eta\) is a homomorphism to \(\mathbb Z_2\) which factors through \(H_1(G,\mathbb Z_2)=(\mathbb Z_2)^r\).

The passage from regime (A) to regime (C) is governed by the edgewise gap, the amount by which the best assignment on an edge beats every competitor.

eq. 5 \[ \Delta(x,x') := \min_{\tau\neq\sigma_{x,x'}} \sum_{i} C_{i,\tau(i)} \;-\; \sum_{i} C_{i,\sigma_{x,x'}(i)} \;\ge\; 0 . \]

Regime (B) splits according to the behaviour of this quantity. When the assignment is unique with \(\Delta \ge \Delta_\star > 0\) uniformly, the coupling concentrates exponentially on the optimal permutation matrix, with \(\|\Pi^{(\varepsilon)}-P_\sigma\|_1 \le K e^{-\Delta_\star/\varepsilon}\) for a constant \(K\) independent of the edge [3]. When the assignment admits several optima, or when the gap vanishes along the temperature sweep, the optimum lies on a face of the Birkhoff polytope rather than at a vertex. The uniform estimate then fails and the rates become polynomial, with mean row entropy of order \(\varepsilon\) and Frobenius distance to a Hungarian optimum of order \(\sqrt\varepsilon\). The limit point in that case is a distinguished interior element of the optimal face and is not a permutation matrix.

The discrete Stokes law

Let \(D\) be a simply connected 2-cell subcomplex with face set \(F(D)\), basepoint \(x_0\) on \(\partial D\), and boundary read counter-clockwise. Fix a spanning tree \(\mathscr T_D\) of the vertices of \(D\) rooted at \(x_0\). For each face \(c\) choose a corner \(v_c\), let \(\pi_c\) be the unique tree path from \(x_0\) to \(v_c\), and set \(g_c := \Hol_{\pi_c}\). An enumeration \((c_1,\dots,c_n)\) of \(F(D)\) is tree-compatible when at each step \(k\) the path \(\pi_{c_k}\) lies inside the boundary of the region \(D_k = D\setminus\bigcup_{j < k}c_j\) not yet peeled. Such an enumeration exists for every pair of tree and region.

In regime (C) the boundary holonomy is the tree-ordered product of the face holonomies, each conjugated to the basepoint.

eq. 6 \[ \Hol_{\partial D} = \prod_{k=1}^{n} g_{c_k}^{-1}\,\Hol_{c_k}\,g_{c_k} \quad\text{in } \SN . \]

The proof is an induction on the number of faces. Peeling the last face of a tree-compatible enumeration leaves a simply connected region, the shared portion of the two boundaries cancels, and tree-compatibility guarantees that the path \(\pi_{c_n}\) already lies in the boundary of the peeled region, with no further homotopy across the interior required. The right-hand side is independent of the tree, of the corners, and of the enumeration, beyond the requirement of tree-compatibility.

The identity restates a standard fact of lattice gauge theory [4] for transports valued in the symmetric group. What the specialisation contributes is the abelianisation, since applying \(\eta\) removes the conjugation factors and with them the tree ordering.

eq. 7 \[ \eta(\partial D) = \prod_{c\in F(D)}\eta(c) . \]

Eq. 7 follows from multiplicativity of the determinant and its invariance under conjugation, together with \(\det\Hol_c \in\{\pm1\}\) for \(\Hol_c\in\SN\). It has the form of the \(\mathbb Z_2\) cocycle observable of Wegner [7].

On a closed oriented surface \(\Sigma\) triangulated by \(G\), every edge is traversed exactly once in each direction by its two adjacent faces. The two contributions of an edge are \(\operatorname{sign}\det T_e\) and \(\operatorname{sign}\det T_e^{-1}\), which are equal, and their product is \(+1\).

eq. 8 \[ \prod_{c\in F(\Sigma)}\eta(c) = +1 . \]

Faces carrying odd holonomy therefore occur in even number on any closed oriented surface.

Regime (A) does not admit eq. 6 verbatim. The reversal relation weakens from \(T_{\beta\alpha}=T_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}\) to \(T_{\beta\alpha}=T_{\alpha\beta}^{\top}\), and interior edges no longer cancel at the level of operators. They cancel up to a squared determinant, and the closed-surface identity acquires a deficit.

eq. 9 \[ \prod_{c\in F(\Sigma)}\det\Hol_c = \prod_{e\in E(\Sigma)}(\det T_e)^{2} \in [0,1]. \]

The right-hand side equals one exactly when every \(T_e\) is a permutation matrix, by Hadamard's inequality for doubly stochastic matrices [6]. It is gauge invariant, and it measures the failure of the doubly stochastic connection to be reversible. In the gapped case \(|\det T_e|\to 1\) exponentially in \(1/\varepsilon\) and eq. 9 recovers eq. 8. In the degenerate case the deficit stays away from one by a polynomial in \(\varepsilon\), and its size measures how far the limit transport is from being permutation valued.

The signature on the torus

Eq. 8 says nothing about the two independent non-contractible loops of the torus, since those loops bound no region. Write \(\gamma_\alpha\) and \(\gamma_\beta\) for the generators of \(\pi_1(T^2,x_0)\cong\mathbb Z^2\) and take the pair of signs they carry.

eq. 10 \[ (\eta_\alpha,\eta_\beta) := \big(\eta(\gamma_\alpha),\eta(\gamma_\beta)\big) \in \mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2 . \]

The pair is invariant under relabelling at the basepoint and under deformation of the generators within their based homotopy classes. Any contractible loop bounds a simply connected region, and by eq. 7 its \(\eta\) is a product of face invariants, insensitive to the values taken on the non-contractible generators. A loop homologous to \(a\gamma_\alpha+b\gamma_\beta\) modulo two satisfies \(\eta(\gamma)=\eta_\alpha^{\,a}\eta_\beta^{\,b}\), and the four values partition the regime-(C) connections on \(T^2\) into sectors that no contractible observable separates.

The codomain is the first \(\mathbb Z_2\)-cohomology of the torus. The universal coefficient theorem applied to \(H_1(T^2,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\) gives

eq. 11 \[ \operatorname{Hom}\!\big(\pi_1(T^2,x_0),\mathbb Z_2\big) \;\cong\; H^1(T^2,\mathbb Z_2) \;\cong\; \mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2 , \]

and \(\Sigma(\rho)=\eta\circ\rho^{\mathrm{ab}}\) is a class in it. All four classes are attained. Multiplying every transport along the \(\alpha\)-seam on the left by a fixed odd permutation multiplies \(\Hol_{\gamma_\alpha}\) on the left by that permutation and leaves \(\Hol_{\gamma_\beta}\) unchanged, which flips \(\eta_\alpha\) and preserves \(\eta_\beta\). The same operation on the \(\beta\)-seam flips the other component, and composing the two reaches every element of \(\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\) from any base connection.

One comparison should be made carefully. The group \(H^1(T^2,\mathbb Z_2)\) acts simply transitively on the set of spin structures of the torus [5], and the four sectors therefore stand in bijection with those spin structures once a base point of the torsor is fixed. The correspondence is one of sets carrying an action of the same group and carries no further structure. The construction supplies no quadratic refinement of the intersection form, no Arf invariant and no Dirac operator, and \(\Sigma\) records the cohomology class alone rather than a spin structure in the geometric sense.
